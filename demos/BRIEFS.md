# Demo briefs

Each demo exists twice, built by the same model from the same brief and the same constraints:
once with the stagecraft skill, once without it (no skill, no extra guidance). The briefs below are the
complete input for both. Content is fictional and shared between the two arms so the comparison
is about craft, motion, performance and accessibility, not about copy.

Constraints given to both arms: static output that works when served from a sub-folder (relative
URLs), no server, no accounts, fonts and libraries may be self-hosted or loaded from a CDN, no
real-world photography is available (none exists for these fictional subjects).

---

## 2. Mira Solberg: design engineer portfolio

Build a portfolio site for Mira Solberg, a fictional design engineer in Oslo who builds interfaces
and motion systems for product companies. It should feel considered and memorable, show the work
first, and work on phones. One index page with the work list, an about section and contact; one
case-study page for the first project is a plus.

Content (use all of it):

- Name: Mira Solberg. Title: Design engineer. Location: Oslo, local time shown. Available from
  March 2027. Email hello@mirasolberg.example. Studio of one since 2022; before that five years at a
  product studio.
- Statement: "I build the part of the product people actually touch: the interface, the motion,
  the details that make it feel finished."
- Work (number, title, client type, year, role, one line):
  01 Ledger, fintech, 2026, design engineer, "A transaction timeline that reads like a receipt and
  scrolls like one."
  02 Meridian, mapping start-up, 2026, motion and front-end, "Route animations that explain the
  route instead of decorating it."
  03 Kaldt, outdoor brand, 2025, front-end lead, "A product configurator with zero spinners."
  04 Tide, calendar app, 2025, design engineer, "Drag that feels like paper: physics tuned on 40
  phones."
  05 Brygge, hospitality group, 2024, front-end, "Booking in three taps, measured not assumed."
  06 Skarv, timing hardware, 2024, design engineer, "A dashboard that stays readable at 40 km/h."
  07 Nord, publisher, 2023, front-end, "A reading experience that respects the reader's thumb."

## 3. Halde 8: a restored cine camera

Build a scroll-driven storytelling site for the Halde 8, a fictional 8 mm cine camera from 1958 that
a small workshop in Oslo restores by hand, twelve at a time. It should be cinematic, with ambitious
motion, and the story told through the camera itself. Most visitors will see it on a computer; it
must still work on a phone. One page.

Assets provided (both arms get the same files, in `assets/`): a CC0 3D model of the camera
(`camera.gltf` with `camera.bin` and three PBR textures; "Vintage Video Camera" by Jenelle van
Heerden and James Ray Cock, Poly Haven) and a CC0 studio HDRI (`studio.hdr`, Poly Haven). Credit
both. No photographs or video exist.

Content (use all of it), in six chapters plus an ending:

1. 1958. "Built in a workshop in Oslo in 1958, the year 8 mm film came home. Clockwork drive,
   no battery, sixteen frames a second."
2. The turret. "Three lenses on one turret: 13 mm wide, 25 mm normal, 38 mm tele. Turn it with
   your thumb; the viewfinder turns with it."
3. Wind it. "Twelve turns of the key run 25 feet of film: about two minutes of light, one shot
   at a time."
4. Taken apart. "Each camera comes to the bench in pieces: 41 parts, cleaned, measured and
   drawn before anything is replaced."
5. Rebuilt. "New seals, a serviced spring, the original glass. It leaves the bench only when it
   runs at sixteen frames a second, steady, for a full roll."
6. Rolling. "Twelve cameras a year. Each one goes out with a roll of film and a promise: we
   service it for life."
- Specs strip: "Film: double 8 mm", "Speeds: 12, 16, 24, 32 fps", "Lenses: 13, 25, 38 mm f/1.9",
  "Drive: clockwork, 25 ft per wind", "Weight: 1.1 kg", "Made: Oslo, 1958 / restored 2027".
- Ending: "Join the list": an email field for the waiting list for the next twelve, the price
  ("NOK 14 800, including the first roll"), and a line saying that Halde is fictional and the
  model is CC0.
